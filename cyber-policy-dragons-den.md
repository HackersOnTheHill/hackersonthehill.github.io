<section class="briefing-section">

      <div class="col-12 col-lg-9">
        <h2>What is it?</h2>
        <p>You know the show. Now swap the investors for influential people from across UK government, and the business plans for public policy ideas to make our connected world safer.</p>
        <p>In the Cyber Policy Dragons' Den, cyber policy innovators pitch solutions to people from across UK government who have the tools, influence, and track record to put ideas into action. Hackers spot problems early. Policymakers can do something about them. The Den shows what works best to get them working together, and gives everyone in the room a look at how policymakers think about the cybersecurity issues facing society.</p>
        <p>The Den is the UK version of the <a href="/cyber-policy-shark-tank/">Cyber Policy Shark Tank</a>, which has run at Hackers on the Hill in Washington, DC since 2025. The format is similar, but the Dragons and the levers they can pull are specific to the UK.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="briefing-section briefing-bg-alt">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-12 col-lg-9">
        <h2>How it works</h2>
        <p>Each finalist has a brief window to cover four things:</p>
        <ul class="briefing-bullets">
          <li><strong>The problem</strong> you want to solve</li>
          <li><strong>A specific step</strong> that can address it</li>
          <li><strong>What to expect</strong> if it succeeds</li>
          <li><strong>What you need from the Dragons</strong> to make it happen</li>
        </ul>
        <p>The Dragons then ask questions, suggest tweaks, and offer to invest their time, energy, and political capital alongside you.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="briefing-section">
  <div class="container">

        <h2>How ideas are judged</h2>
        <p>The winner is the idea the Dragons judge to be the most well-rounded. Ideas are scored on the following system (yes, this goes to 11 &#x1F60E;):</p>
        <div class="briefing-table-wrap">
          <table class="briefing-table">
            <thead>
              <tr>
                <th>Criterion</th>
                <th>Points</th>
              </tr>
            </thead>
            <tbody>
              <tr>
                <td>Names the right levers of power in UK government (formal or informal)</td>
                <td>4</td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Likely to address the problem it sets out to solve</td>
                <td>3</td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Feasible to implement</td>
                <td>3</td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Creativity</td>
                <td>1</td>
              </tr>
            </tbody>
          </table>
        </div>
        <p>The winner takes home fabulous prizes, such as dragon-themed swag, and a chance to work on their idea with cyber policy experts in and around the UK government.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

        <h2>Who are the Dragons?</h2>
        <p>As on the TV show, your goal is to get one or more Dragons to back your idea. Will you try to win over one of them completely, or all of them a little? Will you propose something ambitious, or something small for a quick win? Those choices are yours.</p>
        <p>The Dragons are drawn from several UK government departments, along with former civil servants whose experience lets them speak more freely. They are all people who can help turn good ideas into action. They are not just there to judge; they are there to help your idea succeed. The Dragons will be announced closer to the event.</p>
